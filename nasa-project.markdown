# Mapping Deforestation and Land-Use Transitions in the Amazon Using LiDAR and Remote Sensing

## Overview

This research project examined deforestation patterns, land-use transitions, and forest structural changes in the Brazilian Amazon using LiDAR and remote sensing data.

The project was conducted through a collaboration involving Cal Poly and NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL), with support from SERVIR Amazonia.

The research integrated GEDI LiDAR observations, PRODES land-cover data, GIS workflows, and spatial analysis techniques to investigate relationships between forest canopy structure, agricultre and deforestation.

## Research Questions

- How is the Amazon forest developing in previously deforested areas?
- How is canopy height related to human activity?
- What patterns exist between old-growth and new-growth forest regions?

## Project Workflow

## Project Workflow

This project integrated multiple large geospatial and remote sensing datasets to examine deforestation and forest structural changes across Pará, Brazil.

The workflow combined PRODES land-cover classifications with GEDI LiDAR observations collected from the International Space Station. GEDI point data were filtered for quality metrics and spatially joined with PRODES raster classifications to identify relationships between forest structure, land-use transitions, and road proximity.

Geoprocessing and analysis included:

- Raster processing and reprojection
- Spatial joins and raster sampling
- Data filtering and quality control
- Statistical analysis of canopy height metrics
- Distance-to-road analysis
- Visualization of deforestation and regrowth patterns

Analysis was conducted using ArcGIS Pro, QGIS, and Python-based geospatial workflows.

## Spatial Analysis and Mapping

## Spatial Analysis and Mapping

Spatial analysis focused on identifying relationships between road networks, forest canopy structure, and deforestation dynamics throughout the Brazilian Amazon.

Mapping outputs highlighted:
- fishbone deforestation patterns associated with transportation corridors
- differences between old-growth and secondary-growth forest regions
- canopy height variation relative to road proximity
- spatial clustering of deforestation and regrowth

LiDAR-derived canopy metrics and remote sensing products were used to evaluate structural forest changes and landscape fragmentation across large spatial scales.

The project produced a range of cartographic and analytical outputs, including:
- thematic GIS maps
- regression visualizations
- density and distribution analyses
- canopy height models
- spatial statistical graphics

![Deforestation Map](/assets/images/nasa-project/deforestation-map.png)

## Key Findings

- In years following deforestation, canopy heights continued to decrease
- Forest canopy height generally increased with distance from roads.
- Deforestation patterns were strongly associated with transportation corridors.
- LiDAR and remote sensing methods provided insight into forest degradation and recovery processes.
